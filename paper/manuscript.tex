\begin{abstract}
We determine the supremum of the $I_{3322}$ Bell functional in both the
tensor-product and commuting-operator models.  The value is the rigorously
characterized domain-wall constant
\[
q_*=0.250875384513976536\ldots,
\quad
q_*\in[0.250875384513976535514,0.250875384513976536486].
\]
A computer-assisted shooting argument produces a positive Bellman fixed point,
an $\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$ domain-wall eigenvector, and finite tensor-product
strategies approaching $q_*$.  Installing the wall in the alternating
P\'al--V\'ertesi projector blocks gives an explicit normal spatial strategy
that attains $q_*$.  A
representation-free operator certificate, obtained by geometrically combining
a Bellman inequality with its reflected counterpart, gives the matching
commuting-operator upper bound.  Equality on a finite joint spectrum forces
two local decreasing transports to coincide.  Their amplitude compatibility
then bounds every putative finite attaining component by $1/4$.  Since
$q_*>1/4$, no finite-dimensional tensor-product or commuting-operator strategy
attains the supremum.  Thus the maximizing correlation lies in
$C_{qs}(3,3;2,2)\setminus C_q(3,3;2,2)$.  The displayed decimal is a validated shooting zero,
not an asserted elementary closed form.  As a corollary, the finite-dimensional
quantum correlation set in the three-input binary scenario is not closed; this
is the minimal bipartite binary-output scenario by input counts where
nonclosure and finite/spatial separation can occur.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement of the result}

Let $A_1,A_2,A_3$ and $B_1,B_2,B_3$ be projections.  In the commuting model
they act on one Hilbert space and $[A_i,B_j]=0$.  We use the normalization
\begin{align}
\B={}&-A_2-B_1-2B_2
 +A_1B_1+A_1B_2+A_2B_1+A_2B_2 \notag\\
&-A_1B_3+A_2B_3-A_3B_1+A_3B_2 .                 \label{eq:bell}
\end{align}
This is the inequality introduced by Collins and Gisin
\cite{CollinsGisin2004}.  Its classical bound is zero.  Qubit strategies
attain $1/4$.  P\'al and V\'ertesi constructed increasing-dimensional
strategies approaching approximately $0.250875384514$ and conjectured
finite-dimensional nonattainment \cite{PalVertesi2010}.
Our projector convention agrees term-for-term with their best-response
recursion.  In the standard Collins--Gisin table it is obtained by swapping
the first two settings on both sides; the exact affine conversion to the
dichotomic convention is recorded in the accompanying normalization
concordance.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is a rigorously characterized constant $q_*$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
q_*\in[0.250875384513976535514,0.250875384513976536486] \label{eq:qinterval}
\end{equation}
for which
\[
\omega_{\otimes}(\B)=\omega_{\rm c}(\B)=q_*.
\]
No finite-dimensional tensor-product strategy and no finite-dimensional
commuting-operator strategy attains $q_*$.  A normal vector state on
$\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$ does attain $q_*$.
\end{theorem}

The NPA hierarchy \cite{NPA2008} and its recent ncKKT refinements
\cite{AraujoEtAl2026} provide increasingly sharp numerical bounds for this
problem.  Our proof instead extracts an exact operator certificate from the
limiting domain-wall recursion.  Infinite-versus-finite dimensional
separations and non-closure are known in larger Bell scenarios
\cite{Slofstra2017,ColadangeloStark2018}; the point here is the exact resolution
of the canonical three-setting binary functional.
Mghirbi previously gave proof-carrying exact rational upper and lower
certificates enclosing the value to width below $10^{-9}$
\cite{Mghirbi2026}
(\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21477901}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.21477901}).
That important antecedent explicitly left the exact
supremum, finite-dimensional attainment, and tensor--commuting equality open.
Very recent budgeted moment-selection work continues to use $I_{3322}$ as a
hard NPA benchmark rather than supplying an exact theorem
\cite{FloraEtAl2026}.

\subsection{Minimal binary spatial separation and nonclosure}

Let $C_q(m_A,m_B;2,2)$ denote the set of finite-dimensional bipartite quantum
correlations with $m_A$ and $m_B$ binary measurements.  We allow POVMs; this
is equivalent to the finite-dimensional PVM convention.  Let $C_{qs}$ denote
the same correlations with arbitrary spatial tensor-product Hilbert spaces
and normal states.

\begin{theorem}[Minimal binary spatial separation]\label{thm:nonclosure}
There is a correlation
\[
p_*\in C_{qs}(3,3;2,2)\setminus C_q(3,3;2,2)
\quad\text{with}\quad I_{3322}(p_*)=q_*.
\]
Consequently $C_q(3,3;2,2)$ is not closed.  If either $m_A\leq2$ or
$m_B\leq2$, then $C_q=C_{qs}$ and the common set is compact.  Hence
$(3,3;2,2)$ is minimal, under the coordinatewise ordering of input counts,
for both phenomena among bipartite binary-output scenarios.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Section~\ref{sec:spatial} constructs the asserted $p_*$ explicitly.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} excludes every finite-dimensional attainer, so
$p_*\notin C_q$.  Since $C_{qs}\subseteq\overline{C_q}$, this also proves
nonclosure.  The implication from I3322 nonattainment to this statement was
explicitly anticipated by Dykema, Paulsen, and Prakash
\cite{DykemaPaulsenPrakash2018}; general $C_q\ne C_{qs}$ separations were
proved by Coladangelo and Stark \cite{ColadangeloStark2018}.

For minimality, consider $p\in C_q(2,m;2,2)$.  Simultaneously dilate Alice's
two binary POVMs to two projections.  Jordan's lemma decomposes their common
finite-dimensional space into invariant blocks of dimension at most two.
Alice's block-diagonal measurements make $p$ a convex mixture of the
corresponding block correlations.  After purifying each block state into
Bob's system, Schmidt compression reduces Bob's support to dimension at most
two without changing any probability.  Thus
\[
C_q(2,m;2,2)=\operatorname{conv}(K_m),
\]
where $K_m$ is the compact set of qubit--qubit behaviors with the prescribed
measurements.  The convex hull of a compact subset of a finite-dimensional
space is compact.  The standard inclusions
$C_q\subseteq C_{qs}\subseteq\overline{C_q}$ then force $C_q=C_{qs}$.
More explicitly, the no-signaling affine dimension is $3m+2$, so
Carath\'eodory reduces every behavior to at most $3m+3$ qubit blocks, giving
local dimensions at most $6m+6$.  The argument with the parties interchanged
proves the other case.  Related recent dimension bounds appear in
\cite{Masanes2005,PanahiEtAl2025}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is only a binary-output, bipartite setting-count statement.  It
does not compare scenarios with more outputs, more parties, communication, or
network causal structure.

\section{Certified Bellman datum}

Put
\begin{equation}
b(x)=\frac{\sqrt{1-x^2}}2,
\qquad
d(x,u)=xu+\frac{x-u}{2}-1.                       \label{eq:bd}
\end{equation}
The only computer-assisted input to the analytic proof is the following.

\begin{proposition}[Certified Bellman datum]\label{prop:bellman}
There exist $q_*$ satisfying \eqref{eq:qinterval}, a positive continuous
concave function $F:[-1,1]\to(0,\infty)$, and a strictly increasing map
$P:[-1,1]\to[-1,1]$ such that, with
\[
p(x)=\frac{b(x)^2}{F(x)},
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
p(x)+F(u)\le q_*-d(x,u)                            \label{eq:bellman}
\end{equation}
for every $x,u\in[-1,1]$.  For each $u$, equality holds at the unique point
$x=P(u)$.  Moreover, a sequence of finite-dimensional tensor-product
strategies has Bell values converging to $q_*$.  There also exist a profile
$(c_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\subset(-1,1)$ and a positive normalized vector
$\lambda=(\lambda_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$, with geometric
tails, such that
\begin{equation}
H(c)\lambda=q_*\lambda,\qquad
H_{jj}=d(c_j,c_{j+1}),\quad H_{j-1,j}=b(c_j).       \label{eq:wallEigen}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Equivalently, $F$ is a fixed point of
\begin{equation}
(T_qF)(u)=\min_{x\in[-1,1]}
\left(q+1-\frac x2-(x-\tfrac12)u-\frac{1-x^2}{4F(x)}\right). \label{eq:T}
\end{equation}
The validated construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:bellman} is summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:certificate}.

\section{Normal spatial attainment}\label{sec:spatial}

We now install the certified wall in the alternating block construction of
P\'al and V\'ertesi \cite{PalVertesi2010}.  For $t\in[-1,1]$, set
$s_t=\sqrt{1-t^2}$ and
\begin{equation}
P_A^\pm(t)=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}1-t&\pm s_t\\ \pm s_t&1+t\end{pmatrix},\quad
P_B^\pm(t)=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}1+t&\pm s_t\\ \pm s_t&1-t\end{pmatrix},\quad
R=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.   \label{eq:spatialBlocks}
\end{equation}
These are rank-one orthogonal projections.  On each local copy of
$\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$, partition the standard basis into the two perfect
matchings
\[
E_A=\{(2k-1,2k):k\in\mathbb Z\},\qquad
E_B=\{(2k,2k+1):k\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
On every edge $(j-1,j)\in E_A$, install
\[
A_1=P_A^-(c_j),\qquad A_2=P_A^+(c_j),\qquad B_3=R,
\]
and on every edge $(j-1,j)\in E_B$, install
\[
B_1=P_B^-(c_j),\qquad B_2=P_B^+(c_j),\qquad A_3=R.
\]
Each measurement is therefore an orthogonal direct sum of rank-one
projections and defines a bounded projection on $\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$.

Define the unit vector
\begin{equation}
\psi_*=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\lambda_j e_j\otimes e_j
\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb Z).      \label{eq:spatialState}
\end{equation}
This gives a normal vector state.  For the finite-band real matrices $M,N$
used here,
\[
\langle\psi_*,M\otimes N\,\psi_*\rangle
=\sum_{r,s}\lambda_r\lambda_s M_{rs}N_{rs}.
\]
In the present two-banded case all diagonal sums are dominated by
$\sum_j\lambda_j^2$, while the neighbor sums converge absolutely by
Cauchy--Schwarz.  Direct expansion of \eqref{eq:bell} and
\eqref{eq:spatialBlocks} therefore gives
\begin{align}
\langle\psi_*,\B\psi_*\rangle
&=\sum_j d(c_j,c_{j+1})\lambda_j^2
  +\sum_j\sqrt{1-c_j^2}\,\lambda_{j-1}\lambda_j \notag\\
&=\langle\lambda,H(c)\lambda\rangle=q_* .          \label{eq:spatialValue}
\end{align}
The first identity is independently guarded both on exact-rational open and
endpoint-free fixtures and by a symbolic periodic reconstruction.  The last
identity is \eqref{eq:wallEigen}.  Thus the correlation belongs to $C_{qs}$
and attains the certified wall; finite-dimensional nonattainment places it
outside $C_q$.

\section{Geometric symmetrization}

Apply \eqref{eq:bellman} at $(x,u)$ and $(-u,-x)$.  Since
$d(-u,-x)=d(x,u)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
p(x)+F(u)&\le q_*-d(x,u),\\
F(-x)+p(-u)&\le q_*-d(x,u).
\end{split}                                         \label{eq:twoBellman}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
a(x)=\sqrt{p(x)F(-x)},
\qquad
c(u)=\sqrt{F(u)p(-u)}.                              \label{eq:ac}
\end{equation}
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
$(\sqrt{p(x)},\sqrt{F(u)})$ and
$(\sqrt{F(-x)},\sqrt{p(-u)})$ gives
\begin{equation}
a(x)+c(u)\le q_*-d(x,u).                            \label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
Direct multiplication yields
\begin{equation}
a(x)a(-x)=b(x)^2,
\qquad
c(u)c(-u)=b(u)^2.                                   \label{eq:products}
\end{equation}

\section{Commuting-operator certificate}

Set
\begin{equation}
X=A_1+A_2-\id,\quad Y=A_2-A_1,
\qquad
U=B_1+B_2-\id,\quad V=B_2-B_1.                     \label{eq:XYUV}
\end{equation}
The projection relations imply
\begin{equation}
X^2+Y^2=\id,\quad XY+YX=0,
\qquad
U^2+V^2=\id,\quad UV+VU=0.                         \label{eq:csrels}
\end{equation}
A direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}
\B=G(X,U)+Y(B_3-\id/2)+(A_3-\id/2)V,               \label{eq:reparam}
\end{equation}
where
\[
G(X,U)=XU+X/2-U/2-\id.
\]
Let
\[
\alpha(x)=\frac12-a(x),
\qquad
\beta(u)=q_*-\frac12-c(u).
\]
Because $X$ and $U$ commute, joint functional calculus applied to
\eqref{eq:transport} gives
\begin{equation}
R_0:=\alpha(X)+\beta(U)-G(X,U)\ge0.                 \label{eq:R0}
\end{equation}

Let $J_A$ be the polar sign of $Y$ on the support of $\id-X^2$.  Then
\begin{equation}
Y=2b(X)J_A,
\qquad
J_Af(X)=f(-X)J_A.                                  \label{eq:polarA}
\end{equation}
Put $S_B=2B_3-\id$ and $L_A=\sqrt{a(X)}$.  Cross-party commutation gives
$[J_A,S_B]=0$.  Using \eqref{eq:products},
\begin{equation}
L_AJ_AS_BL_A=b(X)J_AS_B=Y(B_3-\id/2).              \label{eq:factorA}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
R_A&:=\frac12\id-\alpha(X)-Y(B_3-\id/2)\\
&=L_A(\id-J_AS_B)L_A\ge0.                          \label{eq:RA}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The product $J_AS_B$ is a self-adjoint contraction.  Endpoint summands, where
$b(X)=Y=0$, follow by continuity.  The identical Bob construction gives
\begin{equation}
R_B:=(q_*-\tfrac12)\id-\beta(U)-(A_3-\id/2)V
=\sqrt{c(U)}(\id-S_AJ_B)\sqrt{c(U)}\ge0.           \label{eq:RB}
\end{equation}
Adding \eqref{eq:R0}, \eqref{eq:RA}, and \eqref{eq:RB} yields
\begin{equation}
\boxed{q_*\id-\B=R_0+R_A+R_B\ge0.}                \label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
Thus $\omega_{\rm c}\le q_*$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:bellman} supplies a
tensor-product sequence approaching $q_*$, proving equality of both suprema.

\section{Finite-dimensional nonattainment}

Suppose a finite-dimensional strategy attains $q_*$, and let $\psi$ be an
attaining unit vector.  Positivity in \eqref{eq:certificate} implies
\begin{equation}
R_0\psi=R_A\psi=R_B\psi=0.                          \label{eq:kernels}
\end{equation}
The scalar value of $R_0$ at a joint spectral pair $(x,u)$ is
$q_*-d(x,u)-a(x)-c(u)$.  Equality forces equality in both inequalities of
\eqref{eq:twoBellman} and in Cauchy--Schwarz.  Hence every occupied pair obeys
\begin{equation}
x=P(u),\qquad -u=P(-x).                             \label{eq:zeroGraph}
\end{equation}
Let $\Sigma$ be the finite set of occupied $u$-coordinates.  Strict
monotonicity makes $P$ injective.

On the occupied interior support define
\[
K_A=J_AS_B,\quad K_B=S_AJ_B,
\qquad
r_A(x)=\frac{a(x)}{b(x)},\quad r_B(u)=\frac{c(u)}{b(u)}.
\]
The local equations in \eqref{eq:kernels} become
\begin{equation}
K_A\psi=r_A(X)\psi,
\qquad
K_B\psi=r_B(U)\psi.                               \label{eq:localKernel}
\end{equation}
The first reverses $x$ and therefore acts on $\Sigma$ by
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak a(u)=P^{-1}(-P(u)),                      \label{eq:amap}
\end{equation}
while the second acts by
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak b(u)=-u.                                 \label{eq:bmap}
\end{equation}
Both are decreasing bijections of the same finite ordered set.  Such a set
has only one decreasing bijection, so $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak b$.  Every
occupied $(x,u)$ is paired with $(-x,-u)$.

Let $\psi_+$ and $\psi_-$ be the corresponding nonzero spectral components.
Taking norms in \eqref{eq:localKernel} and using \eqref{eq:products} gives
\begin{equation}
\rho:=\frac{\|\psi_-\|}{\|\psi_+\|}
=\sqrt{\frac{F(-x)}{F(x)}}
=\sqrt{\frac{F(u)}{F(-u)}}.                       \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Bellman and Cauchy equality then force
\begin{equation}
F(x)=\frac{b_x}{\rho},\quad F(-x)=\rho b_x,
\quad F(u)=\rho b_u,\quad F(-u)=\frac{b_u}{\rho}. \label{eq:Ffour}
\end{equation}
At the two reflected components,
\begin{equation}
q_*-d(x,u)=\rho(b_x+b_u),
\qquad
q_*-d(-x,-u)=\frac{b_x+b_u}{\rho}.                \label{eq:twoValues}
\end{equation}
Subtracting and eliminating $\rho$ gives
\begin{equation}
q_*=xu-1+\sqrt{(b_x+b_u)^2+\frac{(x-u)^2}{4}}.     \label{eq:qfinite}
\end{equation}

Put $s_x=\sqrt{1-x^2}$, $s_u=\sqrt{1-u^2}$, and $t=1-xu\ge0$.  Then
\begin{equation}
(b_x+b_u)^2+\frac{(x-u)^2}{4}
=\frac{1-xu+s_xs_u}{2}\le t,                      \label{eq:radbound}
\end{equation}
since
\[
(1-xu)^2-s_x^2s_u^2=(x-u)^2\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}
q_*\le-t+\sqrt t\le\frac14,                       \label{eq:quarter}
\end{equation}
contradicting \eqref{eq:qinterval}.  This proves finite-dimensional
nonattainment.  Independently, a finite-dimensional commuting representation
decomposes into finitely many spatial matrix blocks; equality would force one
finite tensor block to attain $q_*$.

\section{Validated construction of the Bellman datum}\label{sec:certificate}

The Bellman equality and stationarity equations close as a three-dimensional
shooting map.  In coordinates $(z,x,r)$, put
\[
\Delta=zx+\frac{z-x}{2}-1,
\qquad
v=\frac{2(q-\Delta-\sqrt{1-z^2}/(2r))}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}.
\]
The successor coordinate is the explicit rational-radical expression obtained
from stationarity.  The selected orbit leaves a hyperbolic positive plateau,
meets a two-condition reflection section, and returns by reversing symmetry.

The validation stack comprises: a 400-bit Miranda enclosure of the shooting
zero; a symbolic envelope-covariance identity; 300-bit interval covers of the
central graph and endpoint wings; strict derivative signs on every tile;
exclusion of all inactive outer predecessors; positivity of the Bellman
function; and square-summable domain-wall tails whose finite truncations
approach $q_*$.  Every computer-assisted item was also reconstructed with a
separate \texttt{mpmath.iv} implementation and locally written complex
intervals, without importing the Arb/FLINT production modules.  All gates pass
and its enclosure overlaps the production result.  The spatial block identity
has two further independent guards: exact rational fixtures, including
endpoint-free controls, and a symbolic polynomial reconstruction.  The
certificate map and
technical supplement give every script, margin, failed guard, and the exact
independence contract.

\section{Scope}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} concerns one Bell functional.  It does not assert an
elementary closed form for $q_*$, spatiality of arbitrary commuting
correlations, uniqueness or self-testing of the maximizer, or experimental
accessibility of the limiting wall.  It separates $C_q$ from $C_{qs}$ at this
correlation but does not separate $C_{qs}$ from $C_{qa}$ or $C_{qa}$ from
$C_{qc}$.  The proof is independent of any broader foundational
interpretation.  The observed finite-carrier gaps are exponentially
consistent with the plateau multiplier, but no quantitative device-independent
dimension lower bound is claimed.
