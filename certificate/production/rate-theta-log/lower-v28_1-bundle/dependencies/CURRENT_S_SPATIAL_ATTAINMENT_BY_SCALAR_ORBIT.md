# Current-\(S\) Spatial Attainment by Scalar-Orbit Extraction

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Status:** **Analytic promotion candidate; independent blind audit required.**  
**Claim if promoted:** the current I3322 supremum \(S\) is attained by a
normal spatial strategy, so

\[
C_{qs}(3,3;2,2)\setminus C_q(3,3;2,2)\ne\varnothing.
\]

This theorem is separate from the promoted finite-dimensional nonattainment
Theorem (N).

The proof uses:

1. the current value

   \[

4. exact endpoint \(R_0\)-gaps;

8. the finite Pál--Vértesi block-to-Jacobi identity.

wall.

\(C^\ast\)-algebra and \(\mathcal B\) its Bell element.

Weak-\(*\) compactness of the state space gives a state \(\omega_\ast\) with

\[
\omega_\ast(\mathcal B)=S.
\]

Let

\[
(\pi,\mathcal H,\Omega)

## 3. Scalar Fatou passage

Put

\[
X=A_1+A_2-I,\qquad U=B_1+B_2-I.
\]

These commute. Let \(\mu\) be their joint spectral measure in \(\Omega\).

Write

\[
R_{0,n}=\phi_n(X,U),
\qquad
\phi_n\ge0.
\]

Because

\[
\sum_\nu
\langle\Omega,R_{\nu,n}\Omega\rangle
=
q_n-S\to0,
\]

we have

\[
\int\phi_n\,d\mu\to0.
\]

On \((-1,1)^2\),

\[
\phi_n(x,u)\to\phi(x,u).
\]

The exact endpoint gaps imply

\[
\mu\{x=\pm1\text{ or }u=\pm1\}=0.
\]

Fatou therefore gives

\[
\int\phi\,d\mu=0.
\]

Since \(\phi\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{\phi=0\quad\mu\text{-almost everywhere}.}
\]

Thus the maximizing scalar measure is carried by the current full-zero locus.

## 4. Correct response-block limit

Define

\[
p_n(x)=\frac{b(x)^2}{g_n(x)},
\]

\[
A_n(x)=\sqrt{p_n(x)g_n(-x)},
\qquad
B_n(u)=\sqrt{g_n(u)p_n(-u)}.
\]

The exact response remainders are

\[
\boxed{
R_{A,n}
=
A_n(X)-b(X)K_A,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
R_{B,n}
=
B_n(U)-b(U)K_B,
}
\]

where \(K_A,K_B\) are self-adjoint unitaries and

\[
K_AXK_A=-X,\qquad K_BUK_B=-U.
\]

These are positive rank-one \(2\times2\) transport blocks, not squared
differences.

Bellman feasibility at the reflected target gives

\[
p_n(x)+g_n(-x)\le q_n-d(x,-x).
\]

Hence

\[
A_n(x)
\le
\frac{p_n(x)+g_n(-x)}2
\le
\frac{q_n-d(x,-x)}2.
\]

Thus \(A_n\) is uniformly bounded; likewise \(B_n\).

Interior pointwise convergence and absence of endpoint atoms give

\[
A_n(X)\Omega\to A(X)\Omega,
\qquad
B_n(U)\Omega\to B(U)\Omega.
\]

Moreover the positive operators \(R_{A,n},R_{B,n}\) have uniformly bounded
norm. Therefore

\[
\|R_{A,n}\Omega\|^2
\le
\|R_{A,n}\|
\langle\Omega,R_{A,n}\Omega\rangle
\to0,
\]

and similarly for \(R_{B,n}\).

Passing to the vector limit yields

\[
\boxed{
K_A\Omega=r_A(X)\Omega,
\qquad
r_A(x)=\frac{A(x)}{b(x)},
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
K_B\Omega=r_B(U)\Omega,
\qquad
r_B(u)=\frac{B(u)}{b(u)}.
}
\]

All divisions occur \(\mu\)-almost everywhere in the endpoint-free interior.

## 5. Scalar Radon--Nikodym laws

Let \(\mu_X,\mu_U\) be the two marginals.

For every bounded Borel \(f\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int f(-x)\,d\mu_X(x)
&=
\langle K_A\Omega,f(X)K_A\Omega\rangle\\
&=
\int f(x)r_A(x)^2\,d\mu_X(x).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
d((-{\rm id})_\ast\mu_X)
=
r_A^2\,d\mu_X.
}
\]

Similarly,

\[
\boxed{
d((-{\rm id})_\ast\mu_U)
=
r_B^2\,d\mu_U.
}
\]

The product identities are exact:

\[
r_A(t)r_A(-t)=1,
\]

\[
r_B(t)r_B(-t)=1,
\]

\[
r_A(t)r_B(t)=1.
\]

## 6. The strict graph and response transformations

The full-zero locus is a one-to-one strictly increasing relation. Write it as

\[
Z=\{(P(u),u):u\in Y\}
\]

on a Borel set \(Y\subset(-1,1)\).

The dual-zero involution gives

\[
P(-P(u))=-u.
\]

Define

\[
a(u)=P^{-1}(-P(u)),
\qquad
b(u)=-u,
\qquad
\tau=a\circ b.
\]

The marginal transport laws become

\[
\boxed{
\mu_U(aE)
=
\int_E r_A(P(u))^2\,d\mu_U(u),
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mu_U(bE)
=
\int_E r_B(u)^2\,d\mu_U(u).
}
\]

The response equivalence relation generated by \(a,b\) is countable.

A fixed point of \(\tau\) is a common-return sector and satisfies the
multiplicity-uniform quarter ceiling. Since \(S>1/4\), the current full-zero
locus contains no state-carrying finite response orbit.

## 7. Explicit Borel transversal

On

\[
Y_+=\{u:\tau(u)>u\},
\]

put

\[
\alpha(u)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb Z}\tau^n(u),
\qquad
\beta(u)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb Z}\tau^n(u).
\]

Choose the first rational \(q_k\) in a fixed enumeration of \(\mathbb Q\) such
that

\[
\alpha(u)<q_k<\beta(u).
\]

Call it \(q(u)\). It is Borel and constant on each \(\tau\)-orbit.

Define

\[
D_+
=
\{u\in Y_+:u\le q(u)<\tau(u)\}.
\]

Every increasing \(\tau\)-orbit meets \(D_+\) exactly once.

Apply the same construction to \(\tau^{-1}\) on

\[
Y_-=\{u:\tau(u)<u\}.
\]

This gives a Borel transversal \(D\) for the \(\mathbb Z\)-action.

The reflection \(b\) induces a Borel involution on \(D\). Choosing the lesser
of the two representatives gives a Borel transversal for the complete
infinite-dihedral response relation.

Therefore the full response relation is smooth.

## 8. Orbit disintegration needs only the scalar measure

Disintegrate

\[
\mu_U
=
\int_T\mu_t\,d\nu(t)
\]

over the response-orbit quotient.

Because \(a,b\) preserve the quotient and the Radon--Nikodym identities are
scalar, uniqueness of disintegration implies that for \(\nu\)-almost every
\(t\), the conditional probability \(\mu_t\) satisfies the same two transport
laws.

No decomposition of the full GNS representation is required.

Choose one conditional orbit measure. It is supported on a countable infinite
response orbit. Since the transport densities are positive and finite, every
point of its support has positive mass.

## 9. Interleaving a characteristic path

Choose \(u_0\) in the selected orbit and put

\[
u_n=\tau^n(u_0).
\]

Define a bi-infinite characteristic label sequence by

\[
\boxed{
c_{2n}=u_n,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
c_{2n+1}=-P(-u_n).
}
\]

The dual-contact identity gives

\[
P(c_{j+1})=c_j
\qquad
(j\in\mathbb Z).
\]

Thus every adjacent pair is a critical Bellman contact.

Define unnormalized amplitudes by

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde\lambda_{2n}
=
\sqrt{\mu_t(\{u_n\})},
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde\lambda_{2n+1}
=
\sqrt{\mu_t(\{-u_n\})}.
}
\]

The two response Radon--Nikodym laws give

\[
\frac{\widetilde\lambda_{2n+1}}
{\widetilde\lambda_{2n}}
=
r_B(c_{2n}),
\]

and, because

\[
a(-u_n)=u_{n+1},
\]

\[
\frac{\widetilde\lambda_{2n+2}}
{\widetilde\lambda_{2n+1}}
=
r_B(c_{2n+1}).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\widetilde\lambda_{j+1}}
{\widetilde\lambda_j}
=
r_B(c_j)
\quad\text{for every }j.
}
\]

The two sets \(\{u_n\}\) and \(\{-u_n\}\) are either disjoint or identical as
\(\tau\)-orbits. Hence

\[
1
\le
\sum_j\widetilde\lambda_j^2
\le
2.
\]

After normalization,

\[
\lambda_j
=
\frac{\widetilde\lambda_j}
{\|\widetilde\lambda\|_2}
\]

is a positive unit vector in \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\), with the same ratios.

This construction also handles a response reflection with a fixed scalar
point; no parity-pairing assumption is needed.

## 10. Exact Jacobi recurrence

On the full-zero locus,

\[
K(t)=1,
\]

so

\[
B(t)=g(t),
\qquad
r_B(t)=\frac{g(t)}{b(t)}.
\]

Define the Jacobi operator with coordinate \(j\) associated to the target
label \(c_j\):

\[
\boxed{
H_{jj}=d(c_{j-1},c_j),
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
H_{j-1,j}=H_{j,j-1}=b(c_{j-1}).
}
\]

This is the Sprint-1295 Jacobi convention, up to a harmless global index shift.

From the amplitude ratio,

\[
\frac{\lambda_{j+1}}{\lambda_j}
=
\frac{g(c_j)}{b(c_j)},
\]

and

\[
\frac{\lambda_{j-1}}{\lambda_j}
=
\frac{b(c_{j-1})}{g(c_{j-1})}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(H\lambda)_j}{\lambda_j}
&=
d(c_{j-1},c_j)
+
\frac{b(c_{j-1})^2}{g(c_{j-1})}
+
g(c_j)\\
&=
d(c_{j-1},c_j)
+
p(c_{j-1})
+
g(c_j)\\
&=S,
\end{aligned}
\]

where the last line is the critical Bellman equality at

\[
P(c_j)=c_{j-1}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
H\lambda=S\lambda.
}
\]

This is obtained from scalar measure transport alone. Operator-fibre
multiplicity has disappeared.

## 11. Spatial reconstruction

Install the finite Pál--Vértesi alternating rank-one blocks along the labels
\((c_j)\), and let

\[
\psi_S
=
\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
\lambda_j e_j\otimes e_j.
\]

The finite block-to-Jacobi identity extends by truncation because
\(\lambda\in\ell^2\) and the Bell operator is bounded. Therefore

\[
\langle\psi_S,\mathcal B_{3322}\psi_S\rangle
=
\langle\lambda,H\lambda\rangle
=
S.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
S\text{ is normally spatially attained}.
}
\]

Together with promoted Theorem (N),

\[
\boxed{
C_{qs}(3,3;2,2)\setminus C_q(3,3;2,2)\ne\varnothing.
}
\]

## 12. Dependency pruning

- a square factorization of \(R_A,R_B\);
- norm convergence of the limiting weld on the whole GNS Hilbert space;
- a compact interior spectral carrier;
- direct-integral decomposition of the complete operator representation;
- classification of operator multiplicity;
- a pre-existing Jacobi alignment;
- historical spatial attainment;
- or historical tail constants.

The full-operator disintegration and fibre-descent gates are replaced by
scalar Radon--Nikodym transport and explicit interleaving.
